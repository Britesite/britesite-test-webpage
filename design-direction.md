# Café Lærke — Design Direction

Brief for the demo site (2026-11-14). Café Lærke is fictional (see `brand.md`); this brief is
written exactly as for a real intake client, so the site exercises the same artifacts.

## 1. The direction in one sentence

A bright corner room seen from the window seat: warm paper white, the green of the front door,
light oak, and a menu you can read standing in the street on your phone. *(hypothesis)*

## 2. Client requirements and business facts

- **Business fact (intake):** the colours the owner names are the green door and the light wood
  tables. The green carries the identity; oak is the warm secondary surface.
- **Business fact (intake):** no logo. The name is set in type; we do not sell logo design.
- **Functional requirement:** opening hours, address and today's essentials are visible on the
  homepage without scrolling on a 390 px phone.
- **Functional requirement:** the menu is a plain, scannable price list (name, one line, price,
  right-aligned figures), readable at arm's length on a phone.
- **Functional requirement:** the demo strip ("Café Lærke findes ikke …") is on every page, quiet but
  legible, and never removed by a redesign.
- **Functional requirement:** every picture is a CC0 photo with no people (demo; a real client's
  own photos would replace them).

## 3. Visual language

**Typography** *(hypothesis)* — Fraunces for headings: a soft, slightly old-style serif with real
warmth at large sizes, used at a light-to-regular weight and with optical size on, never in all
caps. Figtree for body and UI: friendly, open, very legible at 16–18 px, with tabular figures for
prices. Danish letters (æ, ø, å) render in both.

**Color** *(hypothesis)* — Paper `#FBF7F0` background, ink `#2B2520` text (warm, not black),
door green `#2F5D4A` for links, buttons and the header name; oak `#E8D6B9` and a lighter cream
`#F3EADB` for alternating bands and cards; a muted cinnamon `#B4602F` used only for small
"weekend" and "today" labels. Green on paper passes AA for body text; white on green passes for
buttons.

**Layout** *(hypothesis)* — one centred column of 72 rem max, generous side gutters, sections as
calm horizontal bands that alternate paper and cream. Homepage hero is a split: words and the
practical box (hours, address) on the left, the window-seat photo on the right; on a phone the
headline and the practical box come first (hours above the fold), and the photo follows.

**Photography** *(hypothesis)* — natural light, food and room only, no people. Warm, slightly
desaturated; never a dark moody food-blog look. Photos sit in rounded rectangles, not full-bleed,
so the page stays bright.

**Shape** *(hypothesis)* — soft radii (12–20 px) on images and cards, 999 px pills for the small
labels, 1 px oak borders instead of shadows.

**Density** *(hypothesis)* — relaxed: a visitor scans, they do not read. Three to five sections per
page. Body 17–18 px, headings large (2.5–4 rem on desktop).

**Motion** *(hypothesis)* — none beyond link and button colour transitions. No scroll reveals.

## 4. What we're moving away from

none (no old site)

## 5. What we are deliberately keeping

Not applicable (Brand Strength is Moderate, no old site).

## 6. Site-specific cautions

- The weekend cinnamon swirls may be the homepage's one proud moment; they are a small band with a
  label, not a hero takeover.
- Opening hours are text, never only an image or a graphic.
- No fake social proof: no star ratings, no "kunderne siger" quotes, no award badges.
- The photos are stock (CC0). They are not presented as "our kitchen" or "our team"; captions, if
  any, describe the food, not the people who made it.

## 7. Identity and category-legibility tests

- If the site used a dark, high-contrast palette or black food photography, it would be a cocktail
  bar, not this café.
- A visitor identifies the sector within one second by the window-seat photo and the word "Café"
  in the header, on the homepage; on the menu page by the price list itself.
